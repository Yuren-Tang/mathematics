# Five supports and the unresolved 5-CDC frontier

## Status

The universal and cubic five-support goals remain open. No proof is accepted. The present planning disposition is

`EXTERNAL-HELP-FIRST`.

There is no active internal proof epoch and no branch-level candidate is an active theorem authority.

## 1. Equivalent algebraic forms

For a loopless cubic graph, a five-support witness is a five-indexed family of even edge sets in which every edge belongs to exactly two indices. At the unordered binary level this is equivalently:

- an `R_5`-valued flow, where `R_5={ij:1≤i<j≤5}` records the two supports containing an edge;
- a `K_5`-triangle edge labelling, because the three incident roots at a cubic vertex form a triangle;
- an anisotropic flow in `O^-(4,2)`;
- the quadratic cycle-word formulation;
- a cycle-continuous map to the `K_5` cographic target.

These are equivalent formulations of the five-support goal. They are not integral orientation data; the loss of signs is treated in the root-lift chapter.

## 6. Refuted broad scheduler and double-atom boundary

The proposed lexicographic `(L,mu)` scheduler claimed that a disjoint next source step could always be deleted while staying inside a one-atom state space. The claim is false in its declared broad complete-state scope.

In the labelled Heawood certificate, switching the displayed cycle by `25` leaves one atom `1-14:Q_4`. A disjoint literal source NNI at `5-6`, root-valued in the source flow, has returned-label predecessor value

`12+35 = 23+15 = Q_4`,

and creates a second persistent atom `5-6:Q_4`. The output remains connected, simple, cubic, bridgeless, girth five and cyclically five-edge-connected. The one-atom rank `mu` is no longer defined.

The counterexample unit `disjoint-double-atom-obstruction` refutes `one-atom-prefix-scheduler`; it does **not** refute five-CDC or the one-atom endpoint on a narrower genuine reachable image.

## 8. Possible defect-complex frontier

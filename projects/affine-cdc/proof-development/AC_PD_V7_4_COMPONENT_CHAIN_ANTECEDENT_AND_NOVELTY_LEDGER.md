5. source-dart inheritance after active/inactive absorption;
6. contraction of active cycles;
7. a final connector between two distinguished terminal paths forcing a new
   ordered matching;
8. both co-root and zero-parent prescribed-parent corollaries.

The v9 component-chain theorem is therefore not a literal rediscovery of an
already accepted theorem.

## 9. Prior chain-length, path-contraction or inactive-absorption theorem

A systematic search of the exact branch snapshots, their named parents,
supersession ledgers and current corpus found no prior theorem with:

- inactive singleton nodes `I_h`;
- the active/inactive six-row absorption table;
- an inherited stable-edge connector list;
- proof that active-cycle entry/exit cannot share the modified vertex;
- final equal-endpoint matching change;
- both inverse-parent fibres.

The closest uses of “path”, “strip”, “annulus”, “track” and “contraction” are the
history-strip, Tait-surface and Pachner files above.  None is literally or
functorially the component-chain theorem.

### v9 genuine novelty

1. quotienting the **current source graph** by one physical support channel;
2. representing inactive vertices as individual nodes rather than deleting
   them;
3. absorbing an inactive node by a root NNI while preserving its next connector
   dart;
4. retaining one initial shortest chain as a stable connector list;
5. using the final connector between two distinguished paths to force an
   ordered matching change;
6. using the same theorem for co-root marked arcs and zero-parent terminal
   paths.

This is the new load-bearing mathematics.

## 12. Novelty verdict

The component-chain mechanism has genuine antecedents at the level of local
root NNIs, labelled strip gluing and path/cycle reconnection.  Its global theorem
is new because it combines those local moves with a current-graph physical
quotient and an inherited stable connector chain whose final edge is tied to a
specific prescribed-parent terminal matching.

No old theorem is promoted merely by analogy, and every historical negative
boundary remains in force.

## 2. Complete post-pop state

A complete state is the tuple

\[
\mathfrak S=
(G,x,\mathfrak a,R^-,R^+,P,B,\kappa,\sigma,\mathcal D,\alpha,W).
\]

The fields are:

1. `G`: the complete labelled connected cubic source topology at the current
   same-order stage, including the physically glued cap and every exterior
   attachment;
2. `x`: the complete `E_5` edge-value assignment;
3. `a`: either no atom or one **standard co-root atom** on a named central
   stable edge;
4. `R^-`,`R^+`: the two labelled crossed root resolutions of that atom,
   including their forced internal root values;
5. `P`: the literal live prescribed-parent topology and its four ordered active
   darts;
6. `B`: the ordered cap block, cap vertices, cap edge and cap darts;
7. `kappa`: the current route/profile and every terminal flag;
8. `sigma`: support-index transport, with no support switch performed in this
   epoch;
9. `D`: all stable edge and dart identities, including the five exterior darts
   of every overlap collar;
10. `alpha`: the ancestry map to the source history and to the current
    descendant terminal ledger;
11. `W=(f_L,\ldots,f_1)`: the literal finite list of original root-NNI prefix
    obligations still lying between the atom frontier and the source boundary.

No field is inferred from an isomorphism class.  `G`, `P`, `B`, `D`, `alpha`
and every entry of `W` are labelled source objects.

## 3. Standard atom normal form

After the missing-index single-pop row and its displayed root-lowering NNI,
the only persistent non-root edge has value `Q_i`.  At each endpoint its other
two values are disjoint roots summing to `Q_i`.  The two other binary

Thus a standard atom is equivalently:

- one labelled singular four-branch collar;
- one co-root third resolution;
- two crossed root sheets;
- one ordered choice of the live prescribed parent, independent of which
  crossed sheet is used as a root boundary.

The atom does not itself assert that the prescribed parent is root-valued.
That question belongs to the later root/co-root/zero table.

## 4. Source word versus normalization word

The scheduler keeps two words separate.

### 4.1 Literal source word

`W` is the immutable suffix of the actual root-NNI prefix constructed before
the first cancellation.  Its head is the **only** source obligation that may
be crossed next.  A source step is consumed only when the atom collar has been
transported to the other-order side of that exact labelled NNI, or when a
complete root endpoint or exact terminal is returned.

### 4.2 Local normalization word

Inside one overlap collar the construction may use a bounded word of:

- root NNIs on the fixed five exterior darts;
- one standard co-root relocation;
- at most one transient two-co-root overlap followed immediately by the
  retained root-lowering normalization;
- identity subdivision;
- the retained six-port seam or constant-pivot strip when the completed finite
  comparison is compiled.

These moves do **not** delete an entry of `W`.  They are local comparison cells,
not substitute source progress.

This distinction is the central scheduler invariant.

## 7. Exact quantifier repair

The retained v7.2 cellwise theorem has the form

\[
\forall C\;
[\operatorname{FiniteComparison}(C)
 \wedge \operatorname{NormalizedBoundary}(C)]
\Longrightarrow
\exists R\;\operatorname{RootRectangle}(C,R).
\]

It does **not** imply

\[
\forall\mathfrak S\;\exists C\;
\operatorname{FiniteComparisonFrom}(\mathfrak S,C).
\]

The missing existential comparison is supplied here by the prefix-directed
scheduler:

\[
\forall(\mathfrak S,W)\;
\operatorname{PostPopState}(\mathfrak S,W)
\Longrightarrow
\exists C\;
\begin{cases}
C\text{ is a finite source-faithful comparison},\\
C\text{ has a complete crossed root short side},\ \text{or}\\
C\text{ ends at an exact current-descendant terminal}.
\end{cases}
\]

Only after this construction terminates is the retained v7.2 theorem applied
once to compile the already finite atom track into a root rectangle.  Track
erasure is therefore a terminal compilation step, never the scheduler's
decrease.

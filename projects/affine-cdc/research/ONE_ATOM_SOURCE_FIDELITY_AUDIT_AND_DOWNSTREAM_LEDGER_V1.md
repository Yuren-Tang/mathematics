## 1. Stable dart ledger

### 1.1 Disjoint square

The two source replacements have disjoint vertex supports.  Each fixes the
other's exterior darts.  Their composite dart map is independent of order.
The atom stable edge and the four ordered darts of the literal source NNI
survive with the same identities.

### 1.2 Identical support

The two vertices and four exterior darts are fixed.  Only the internal pairing
is changed to the literal predecessor pairing.  The output is therefore the
exact labelled predecessor, not an isomorphic copy.

### 1.3 Five-leaf overlap

The overlap carrier has five named exterior darts.  Every topology in the local
`C5/C6` table is a two-matching of those same five labels.  Cherry sums force
all internal root values.  Each NNI uses the retained source contract:

- the central stable edge is replaced;
- the two retained branch darts stay at their source endpoints;
- the two moved branch darts carry their stable ids to the opposite displayed
  endpoint;
- all five exterior darts are fixed pointwise.

The entry and exit atom edges are defined by the two operation orders on those
same five labels.  Reaching the exit therefore certifies literal source-step
crossing.

### 1.4 Bad overlap normalization

The transient state `(Q_i,Q_j,ij)` has two adjacent co-root edges on three
source vertices.  The retained root-lowering NNI removes one defect edge while
fixing the exterior boundary.  It is included inside the local cell and cannot
introduce a second ancestry branch.

## 2. Parent, cap and support fidelity

The focused scheduler performs no support switch.

The live parent field consists of the exact predecessor pairing plus its four
active stable darts.  Local atom transport changes neither that record nor its
order.  It is inspected only after a complete crossed root endpoint has been
obtained.

The cap is physically part of the complete cubic graph.  Its vertices, cap
edge and ordered cap darts are retained unless one is literally a named dart
of the current source NNI, in which case the NNI contract records its moved
endpoint without changing its stable identity.

Support transport is the composition of the forced root labels on local
cherries and the pre-existing global index map.  No local choice permutes the
five indices.

## 3. Route/profile discipline

After every root source move and every completed local macro, recompute the
ordered cap route/profile on the current labelled graph.

Exactly one of:

1. the blocked route persists and scheduling continues;
2. the state enters `K_i` or another cap-compatible profile and returns a
   supplied current-descendant terminal;
3. a separating support channel is exposed and returns its exact terminal;
4. a graph-category test returns a named cut/bounded terminal.

A route change is never renamed as atom progress and is never postponed until
after another source step.

## 4. Exact terminal tuple

Every terminal is returned as

\[
(H,\lambda?,B,\kappa,\mathcal C,\alpha),
\]

where:

- `H` is the exact current descendant or completed shore;
- `lambda?` records whether the root flow is already supplied or belongs to a
  strictly-smaller consumer;
- `B` is the ordered cap boundary;
- `kappa` is the current route/profile;
- `C` is one named route, cyclic `2/3/4`-cut, loop, parallel, triangle, theta,
  direct-matching or acyclic low-port class;
- `alpha` is the identity of surviving darts into the stored source prefix.

The one-atom scheduler creates no new terminal class.

## 5. Answers to the #84 missing-theorem attacks

### Attack 1 — where is the finite continuation comparison constructed?

It is constructed by induction on the literal source word.  The head source
step determines the next bounded square/coincident/pentagon macro.  No
existential comparison is assumed.

### Attack 2 — how is the prescribed parent retained?

The parent is a field on every complete state and every local boundary.  The
scheduler ends at a crossed root endpoint carrying that field.  It does not
claim the endpoint topology is already the parent.

### Attack 3 — what is the well-founded scheduler?

\[
(L,\mu),
\]

where `L` is the number of remaining literal source steps and `mu<=3` is the
distance to the fixed exit atom edge in the active labelled five-leaf cycle.
A local path is fixed once and cannot be reselected while `L` is unchanged.

### Attack 4 — why is v7.2 no longer only a supplied-comparison result?

Because the new scheduler supplies the comparison from every post-pop state.
The v7.2 theorem is invoked only after construction terminates.

### Attack 5 — is track erasure counted as prescribed-parent progress?

No.  It compiles the finite comparison into a root rectangle after `(L,mu)`
has terminated.  The parent value is evaluated only afterwards.

## 7. Why no global coherence theorem is needed

The old source left open whether every arbitrary closed rescheduling loop is
generated by disjoint squares and five-leaf circuits.  This epoch does not
prove or use that statement.

There is one already ordered finite source word.  At each stage:

1. its head step is fixed;
2. the local overlap type is fixed;
3. the other-order exit atom edge is fixed;
4. a bounded path of length at most three is fixed;

This is word-directed rewriting, not confluence of arbitrary histories.  No two
global histories are compared and no closed global loop is searched.

The exact local `C5/C6` classification is used only to certify one bounded
overlap macro.
